  Failure modes and decisions
  All TVL in one contract. A bug in the curve is a bug against every launch. Keep the curve to constant product, no exponents, no density functions. Bunni's exploit was rounding in a fancier curve's withdrawal math.
  Aave illiquid on a sell. Withdraw reverts when the market's idle USDC is below the request. Check USDC.balanceOf(aMonUSDC) first and revert with a named error. Only bites when one sell wants a meaningful slice of Monad's
  whole USDC lending market.
  Exact-output swaps. Need the curve inverted plus Aave rounding. Reject them, as the existing aToken wrap hook does, or over-withdraw by a wei and eat the dust.
  Settle a wei short. aToken transfers can credit one wei under the figure sent. The hook only ever settles USDC and the launch token, both exact-transfer, so the wrap hooks' rounding paths do not recur. Assert it anyway.
  Quoting. The V4Quoter runs the real swap in a reverting simulation, hook included. The virtual reserve must live in hook storage, not in a view the quoter cannot reach.
  Gas. Every swap that crosses the buffer pays for one Aave call. Fine on Monad, worth stating in the docs.
  Index-based yield vs. buffer. The buffer is raw USDC and earns nothing. Keep it small, a few percent of principal, and refill it from buys before touching Aave.
  Dormant coin's remaining holders lose their drift. They can revive it with one swap. Say so on the coin page.
  Redirect gaming. Pro rata by fees makes wash trading cost more than the pot. Re-check the math if the fee ever drops or the pot ever gets large.

› explain the issue


• Normally, a Uniswap pool holds the liquidity and records the price. Trading apps read that information to show “$50,000 liquidity,” draw charts, and find trading routes.

  In our proposed design, the hook holds the money and calculates the price itself. Uniswap still handles settlement, but its standard pool has no normal liquidity position.

  So there are two different views:

  - Our hook: “I have USDC invested in Aave and tokens available to trade.”
  - An app reading standard Uniswap pool data: “This pool has zero liquidity.”

  A router that understands the hook could execute trades successfully. But another app might exclude the pool before even trying a trade, show no price, or produce incorrect charts. Tools looking for a locked LP position also
  wouldn’t find one.

  The issue isn’t missing money—it’s that the money and pricing live somewhere existing tools may not understand. We can make our own website understand it. Getting outside trading apps, chart sites, and scanners to understand
  it may require their cooperation. That’s why a technically working design could still be a dead end for the launchpad.
